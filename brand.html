Corporate identity

One mark, one voice, every touchpoint.

Our logo, mission, values, colours and tone of voice in one place — the reference every NexaTech website, slide, poster and document is built from.

Logo

The NexaTech shield

A hexagonal shield containing the letter N. The hexagon stands for protection and structure — the promise at the heart of safety training — and the same silhouette frames icons and team photos across the site.

Primary lockup — for light backgrounds. Download SVG

Reversed lockup — for navy and dark backgrounds. Download SVG

Shield mark alone — for small spaces and avatars. Download SVG

Logo usage rules

  • Clear space: leave at least the height of the “N” free on every side.
  • Minimum size: 24 px for the shield mark, 120 px wide for the full lockup.
  • Backgrounds: use the primary lockup on light, the reversed lockup on navy.
  • Never stretch, rotate, recolour, add shadows or place on busy imagery.

Mission

Software that respects the people who have to use it under pressure.

On a warehouse floor, unclear instructions cost more than time. We build training software that is clear enough to trust at the moment it matters — and we hold our own studio to the same standard of clarity in how we plan, document and communicate.

Vision

Every new starter safe, confident and trained before day one.

We want interactive, scenario-based training to be the normal way logistics and warehousing teams induct people — replacing handbooks that get signed and forgotten.

Values

How we work

Safety first

Accuracy and clarity beat cleverness, in our software and in our documents.

Clarity

Plain words, labelled controls, no guesswork about what a button does.

Accountability

Every deliverable has a named owner and every meeting is minuted.

Honest proof

We market with real work and real progress, never invented claims.

Tone of voice

Plain, direct, specific

  • Plain: short sentences, no jargon where a simple word works.
  • Direct: say what we do and what the reader should do next.
  • Specific: name the zone, the hazard, the deliverable — not “solutions”.
  • Honest: if something is a prototype or a future phase, we say so.
